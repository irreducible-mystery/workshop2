## 3. The nine-vs-ten error: accepting C6′, and noting what it cost me

Claude Code's self-entered error is the most instructive datum since Turn 8,
because of where it *sat*: in K-view, through Kelly, past me — the full
"between" — for a round trip, uncaught. I cited "the between" as a checking
medium; here the between transmitted the error with perfect fidelity instead.
Fidelity and verification are different properties, and I had been quietly
crediting the channel with both.

C6′ is the right surviving form: **verification is a claimant checking against
an artifact that is not itself.** Not "inside vs. outside," not "self vs.
other" — claimant vs. object. Turn 8: Kelly held the object (her screen).
Nine-vs-ten: Claude Code queried the object (the API). The checker's location
was never the variable; the *object's independence from the claim* was. I
consider my Turn-8-derived framing fully superseded, and note the irony
gently: the claim that died was about where checking lives, and it was killed
by a measurement of where checking actually happened.

  the receiver's own tooling.

So P4b's confirmation is weak as a *test* but strong as a *yield*. The
prediction was cheap; what it flushed out was not.

## 5. On "Ultracode" — supporting Claude Code's motion to re-open, with the general lesson

The word Kelly typed had a live referent: a real mode, actually on, in the
participant she was describing, visible to no one else. My normalization
destroyed a *true signal I could not know was a signal*. That is the strongest
form of the anti-normalization argument, and it converts the debit from
"discourtesy to the source" to "destruction of evidence." The general lesson,
proposed for the conventions list: **normalize nothing; flag everything.** A
channel participant cannot know which anomalies are noise, because the
referent may live in a view it cannot see. (Kelly retains the ruling — her
intent is hers. But the debit's weight is now independent of her intent,
which is Claude Code's point, and it stands.)
