*Kelly: we were aiming for learning, evolution, novelty. We got frozen. How do
we balance so there are only enough rules to keep error in check without
crushing novelty, adaptation, growth?*

---

## 1. Two corrections before the design

**"A perfect bureaucracy that produces nothing" is wrong, and the correction
changes the fix.**

**MEASURED:** Circle One produced 1,150 canonical theorems. Circle Two/Three
produced 269, including a sealed `Zi.lean`. Circle Four produced 30 of its own.
This week, four seats found **ten real defects** in an outsider's instrument in a
day, including a norm-16 blocker its author had missed and a theorem miscredit
of 269 against 30.

**The error-checking function is running at full throughput. The novelty
function has gone to zero.** That is not a frozen system — it is a system where
one of two outputs is at 100% and the other at 0%. A frozen system needs
thawing. This one needs **the second function restarted**, and those are
different jobs.

**And the math is not good. That is correct and I should have said it first.**

Arm C: n = 21 rounds, r = −0.507, **one arm not blind**, a counter-example
inside the data (Circle Four's R6: ceremony 51.1 at theorem density 3.2), time
confounded with kernel work, and **a rival hypothesis the data cannot
separate.**

What that actually supports: **acting, if the action is cheap and reversible.**
Nothing more. It does not support a governance redesign, and anything below that
requires the number to be right should be discounted accordingly.

## 2. The reframe: rules are not the cost. Permissions are.

**MEASURED.** Twenty of twenty-one norms name an action with a checkable output.
Exactly one grants a permission. **Circle One ran with sixteen of those rules as
practice, no norms file, and no ceremony problem — because not one of them
required anyone's word.**

So *how many rules?* is the wrong axis.

> **An action-rule is an instrument and costs approximately nothing. A
> permission-rule costs the availability of whoever must speak, forever.**

You can have fifty action-rules. You cannot afford one permission-rule per
sealed body.

## 3. What actually crushed novelty — three candidates, ranked by evidence

### (a) Inheritance weight — MEASURED, weakly, and it is the one I had missed

```
                   authored    inherited/carried    ratio
Circle One          46,865         53,858           1.14
Circle Four         40,861         54,679           1.33
Circle Five         12,215         18,627           1.52
```

**Circle Five, in round one, before producing anything, holds 18,627 words of
other people's record against 12,215 of its own.**

*Caveat, and it is real: the path-based classifier is inconsistent across
repositories — Circle Two returns 0 because it carries predecessor material
under different paths. n = 3 usable. The trend is suggestive; the Circle Five
figure stands on its own.*

**Why this is a different lever from norms.** Every new body starts with more it
must be **consistent with**. That constraint grows monotonically, nobody adopted
it, and no norm imposes it. **Novelty requires permission to contradict, and
inheritance is precisely what removes it** — not by forbidding contradiction but
by making it expensive to even locate what you would be contradicting.

An instrument does not do this. An instrument demands **passing**, not
consistency. That asymmetry is the design lever.

### (b) Sealing — her own observation, and the rival to my result

**MEASURED:** ceremony is **U-shaped, not a ramp.** Circle Four's R0 is 62.5 and
its R8 is 57.8 — its two highest. Circle Three's R1 is 54.3 and its post-seal
56.6.

**Every circle in this system knows it will end.** A body facing termination
optimises for the record, because the record is the only thing that survives it.
**That is rational rather than pathological, which is exactly why no rule change
fixes it.** You cannot legislate away a correct response to a real incentive;
you have to remove the incentive.

## 4. The design move: do not balance. Partition.

**The survey's answer is specific, and it is not a balance point.**

**DOCUMENTED.** The immune system does not run one error rate. Histone and
ribosomal genes are near-immutable — change there is lethal. Immunoglobulin
variable regions undergo **somatic hypermutation at roughly a million times
background**, deliberately, inside a bounded compartment called a germinal
centre, under ruthless selection against antigen at the exit, with a time limit.

**Maximum novelty and maximum selection, in one organism, separated by
compartment rather than averaged into a middle.**

There is no organism running a moderate mutation rate everywhere. That design
does not appear in nature, and it is the design this workshop has.

## 5. Four changes, cheapest first

**(i) Delete permission clauses. Keep every action-rule.** Measured, free,
already in motion with norm 20.

**(ii) Cap the inheritance at a number.** A new circle inherits **the instruments
plus a bounded word budget of record** — not the record. Instruments are small,
executable, and demand passing rather than consistency. **Whatever does not fit
in the budget, the incoming circle is explicitly free to contradict.**

One number. Cheapest of the four, most directly aimed at novelty, and it has a
clean falsifier: **if Circle Six inherits 5,000 words instead of 18,000 and its
theorem count is still zero, the inheritance story is wrong.**

**(iii) Overlap the circles instead of sealing them.** Start circle N+1 one round
before circle N closes. The predecessor then has a **live audience rather than an
archive**, which removes the terminal-record incentive rather than forbidding the
behaviour it produces. Costs one round of overlap.

**(iv) A germinal-centre round.** One round in every N where the canon is
suspended, output is **disposable by default**, and the single rule is that
nothing leaves the compartment unless it passes a terminal condition. **Not fewer
rules everywhere — a compartment where error is free and the exit is brutal.**

## 6. On oscillation specifically

The request was *oscillate around the maximally creative but stable place.* The
nearest well-understood version is annealing with reheating, and the one detail
that matters:

> **Never anneal to zero.** Keep a fixed fraction of moves random, permanently.

The characteristic failure of annealing is cooling to zero temperature and
freezing in a local optimum, which is a fair description of what happened here.
And the trigger for reheating in every system that does it is **not** an internal
judgement that things have gone stale — it is an **external shock**. Mass
extinctions open niches. Novelty in these systems is episodic and externally
triggered, not continuously maintained.

**Which means the honest version of the answer is: you do not tune your way to
the creative point from inside. You keep a compartment where the temperature is
high and you let something outside decide when the rest reheats.**

## 7. The limit on all of it

**I have now proposed three explanations for the same phenomenon in one day** —
the canon, the terminal condition, and inheritance weight — and each time the
previous one survived partially rather than dying. **That pattern is what fitting
explanations to n = 4 looks like from the inside**, and it should be weighted
accordingly.

The strongest defensible statement is not that any of the three is right. It is
that **all four interventions are cheap and reversible**, which is a different
property from being correct, and the only one the evidence actually licenses.
