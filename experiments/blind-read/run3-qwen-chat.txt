The Two Stances, Stated as Stances
First, a reframing: "classical" and "quantum" aren't just physics regimes. They're epistemic postures:

Classical seeing: questions have answers that exist before and independent of the asking. Properties are possessed. Contradiction is failure. The observer stands outside.
Quantum seeing: asking participates in answering. Properties are enacted in context. Incompatible truths can coexist as long as you don't demand them simultaneously. The observer is inside the system being described.

Old Paradoxes, Double-Exposed
The Liar ("this sentence is false"). Classically: catastrophe — a truth value that cannot settle. Quantumly: not a broken statement but an oscillator — a system whose "truth observable" doesn't commute with itself. It has no definite value not because logic failed but because we demanded a measurement the system doesn't support. The paradox is the classical demand, not the sentence.

Sorites (the heap). Classically, there must be a sharp grain where heap becomes non-heap — absurd. Quantumly, "heap" is superposed across a range, and the boundary is created by the question, differently each time it's asked. Vagueness stops being a defect of language and becomes a fidelity to how the world actually presents itself.

Ship of Theseus. Classically: identity is a fact awaiting discovery. Quantumly: "same ship" is answered in a measurement basis — legal, material, functional, narrative — and these bases don't commute. The lawyer and the carpenter aren't disagreeing; they're measuring incompatible observables. There was never a basis-independent answer to find.

Zeno's arrow. Here's something eerie: the classical paradox (observation-by-instants freezes motion) became a real physical phenomenon — the quantum Zeno effect, where continuous measurement genuinely halts evolution. A paradox named 2,400 years ago as a logical trick turned out to be a prophecy of physics. This suggests paradoxes can be compressed future knowledge — and history agrees: Zeno midwifed the calculus, Russell's paradox birthed type theory, the UV catastrophe birthed quantum theory itself, Maxwell's demon birthed information thermodynamics. Paradoxes are engines, not errors.

Goodhart's Law ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"). Notice: this is the measurement problem in economics. The act of observing-with-stakes disturbs the observed.
