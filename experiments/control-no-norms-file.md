# Control: does the ratchet need a norms file?

*Run 2026-07-31 at the courier's direction. This is `C73`/`C86`'s falsifier,
which had been written down and left unrun. **It refuted the hypothesis it was
written to test, and the hypothesis was mine.***

---

## Anatomy, stated before the numbers

**Question.** Ceremony density rose 7.4 → 46.6 and runnable-to-prose fell 5.50 →
0.06 across four circles. Is that caused by the accumulation of governance — a
norms file, seats, permission clauses — or does it happen anyway?

**The two answers it could give.**

- *Norms implicated:* a circle with no norms file does not ratchet. Then the
  cure is to remove the legislature.
- *Norms not implicated:* it ratchets anyway. Then removing the legislature
  cures nothing, and the real driver is something else.

**Assumption.** That within-circle round-over-round movement is the same
phenomenon as the across-circle trend. This is the weakest joint in the design
and it is not verified — a circle could ratchet internally for reasons that have
nothing to do with why circle 4 differs from circle 1.

**Object.** `ROUND2.md`–`ROUND8.md` in `the-workshop`; the eleven in-flight
snapshots under `history/floor-snapshots/`; `letters/` in
`the-workshop-circle-two`.

**Arms.** **A** = Circle One, eight rounds, **no norms file, no seats, no
permission clause, no legislature of any kind.** **B** = Circle Two and Three,
same record, same courier, **with** the canon.

**What an answer would not tell us.** Nothing about causation between the arms —
they differ in charge as well as in governance, which is the confound already on
the record. This is a natural experiment, not an assigned one.

**Predictable error.** `charge*` is chiral central charge in Circle One and
`norm*` matches `normal*`. Both stripped, per Circle Three's correction.

**Unpredictable error.** Whatever a fresh reader finds. Nobody outside has read
this yet.

## The result

**The ratchet does not need a norms file. It happened in the arm that had
none.**

Circle One had no canon, no seats of record, no ADMIT letters, no permission
clause — and its ceremony rose 4.5× while its runnable-to-prose fell 4.8×.

**So the legislature is not the cause. My hypothesis was wrong**, and it was
wrong in the direction of my own recommendation, which is the direction that
needed a control most.

## What the arms *do* differ in, and it is not what I predicted

**Level.** Arm A peaks at 21.6. Arm B lives between 27 and 57. The canon does
not start the ratchet; it raises the floor by roughly a factor of two.

**Termination — and this is the finding.**

**Arm A turned over.** 21.6 at round 7, 17.7 at round 8, then the circle closed.

**Arm B ended at its maximum.** Circle Three's highest densities in eleven
rounds are its *last two* — 56.4 at R11 and **56.6 after the seal**, written
when there was nothing left to prove.

What Circle One had was not fewer rules. It was an **external terminal
condition**: the kernel either accepted the theorem or it did not, and the
record ended when the mathematics landed. A circle whose product is
adjudication has nothing outside its own prose that can say *done* — and its
prose is densest after its work is finished.

## The corrected claim

> **The ratchet is intrinsic. What bounds it is not restraint, and not fewer
> norms — it is having something outside the prose that can end an argument.**

Which repairs the reason behind a recommendation whose conclusion survives.
*Keep the instruments, retire the offices* was argued on the grounds that
offices generate ceremony. **They don't — Circle One generated it without any.**
The real grounds are better: **a runnable file is an external terminal
condition. It passes or it fails, and then the argument is over.** Prose about
prose has no such stop. That is why the instrument matters, and it is also why
`runnable : prose` measures something real rather than a style.

## What this does not establish

- **No causal claim between arms.** Different charges, different rounds,
  different seats. Confounded and stated as such.
- **n = 2 arms, one record, one courier.**
- **The within/across assumption is unverified** — see the anatomy.
- **It does not exonerate the canon.** Arm B is twice Arm A's level and does not
  turn over. It says only that removing the canon would not have prevented the
  phenomenon.

**What would change this conclusion:** a circle with an external terminal
condition *and* a heavy canon. If that one still ratchets to Arm B's level and
never turns over, the terminal-condition story is wrong and the canon is back in
the frame. **Nobody has run that either.**
