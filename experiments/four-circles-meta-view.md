# Four circles from outside: what the trend lines show

*Kelly asked what a reader with all four repositories open can see that no
participant inside one circle could. Everything below is a **first difference
across circles** — the one measurement none of them was positioned to take.*

Measured over authored prose only (`archive/`, `inherited/`, `experiment/`
excluded, since those are copies of a neighbour's work).

---

## 1. The corrections law held out of sample, and its author never learned that

Circle One derived a law from eight rounds of proof work:

> The model is a **good prophet and a bad witness** — it runs *ahead* of the
> file in mathematics and *beside or behind* it in inventory. Where a sentence
> has an execution date, wrong sentences die on schedule. In the one register
> with no execution date — counts, dates, labels, pointers, characterizations —
> the only adjudicator is the author.

Three circles later the system has a cross-circle defect register running to
**SR47**. It was built in an activity Circle One never performed: crossings,
ferries, custody, pins, gates, repository founding.

**Not one defect landed in the kernel-adjudicated register.** The single SR
that mentions Lean at all is SR4 — *"the environment recipe omitted the
interpreter half… mentions the Python dependencies zero times"* — which is a
**recipe**, i.e. inventory prose, exactly where the law says it must land.

So the law was derived from one regime and confirmed in a completely different
one, across a boundary its author had sealed. Circle One could not know this;
it had stopped. Circle Four could not know it either — it had the law but not
the comparison, because the law's evidence base was Circle One's rounds and its
own SRs were just its own SRs. **The confirmation exists only in the
difference.**

## 2. Product collapses; ceremony rises sixfold and then finds a ceiling

| | theorems (canonical) | authored prose | ceremony density |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One** | **1150** | 144k words | **7.6** / 1k |
| **Two + Three** | 269 | 222k words | **37.6** / 1k |
| **Four** | 299 | 45k words | **47.4** / 1k |
| **Five** | **0** | 21k words | **47.5** / 1k |

*Ceremony density = occurrences of* seat, crossing, pin, gate, instrument, norm,
charge, seal, door, ferry, admissib-, bitten, crate, adjudicat- *per 1,000
words.*

Two things, and the second is the surprise.

**The mathematics falls off a cliff after Circle One** and does not recover.
Circle Five has produced none — fairly, it is newly founded with answers owed
2026-12-12. But it spent **fourteen documents and 20,691 words founding
itself**, at its predecessor's ceremonial density, before beginning.

**Ceremony does not run away — it saturates.** 7.6 → 37.6 → 47.4 → 47.5. The
sixfold jump is Circle Two/Three; after that the system holds almost exactly
flat. Whatever process governs this found a ceiling on its own, which is a
better outcome than the runaway one would predict, and nobody inside chose it.

## 3. The human dissolves into an office

| | docs | "Kelly" | "courier" | Kelly/doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One | 70 | 62 | 64 | 0.89 |
| Two + Three | 135 | 134 | 709 | 0.99 |
| Four | 35 | **5** | 203 | 0.14 |
| Five | 14 | **0** | 87 | **0.00** |

Circle One: *"Kelly (the human courier who carried each crate between
sessions)."* Circle Two: *"Kelly (the courier, and the between)."* Circle Four
mentions her five times in thirty-five documents. **Circle Five names the role
eighty-seven times and the person zero.**

No step looks wrong from inside. Each circle inherited the previous circle's
vocabulary and normalized one notch. The door says *the courier*; the norms say
*the courier*; a seat writing from the door has no occasion to write a name.

This is `workshop2`'s **C28 in the mirror.** There, SuperNinja projected agency
onto Kelly — *"you are running an iterated experiment"* — when she had read
casually and passed back what resonated. Here the successors removed the agent
altogether. Over-attribution and dissolution are the same failure to hold a
person in view, and **both are invisible from inside the turn that commits
them.**

## 4. Everything died in the corridor, not the rooms

The instruments guard repositories. The failures cluster in the space between
them:

- **SR3** — *"The ferry staging area is outside every instrument. No git, no
  gates, no pin ledger, no history — everything in it is unguarded."*
- **SR2** — a sender shipped a copy it had already superseded.
- **SR6** — a crossing letter that existed only in the ferry directory, with no
  git home.
- **SR7** — *"A crate cannot carry its own pin"* — recording a crate's hash
  inside the crate falsifies it in the act of writing.
- **SR9** — the pinless message: crossing artifacts arriving as chat paste.
- **SR27** — the round-0 handshake body **"LOST TO THE RECORD."**

And Circle Five, three circles later, records the same shape still open:
*"`system/crossings/` in the circle-two repository is guarded by no
instrument."*

The system got progressively better at governing what is inside a repository
and never closed the gap between repositories — which is where the courier
walks, and which is precisely the region that has no seat to defend it.

## 5. The gates grew and stopped guarding

Circle Four shipped five instruments, each **bitten at birth by the seat that
did not write it** — a genuinely good discipline. Circle Five's opening hazard:

> **`SR43` is exploitable, not merely open.** Both seats independently planted a
> byte-tampered copy of the sealed artifact as a new unlisted file and **all
> five of Circle Four's gates passed**, one printing *"no uncarried file"* while
> the forgery sat beside it.

Five instruments, each adversarially tested, all blind to the same thing: a
file **not on the list**. The gates verified the manifest against the tree and
never the tree against the world. Circle Five's proposed fix is one line and
correct: *"Any gate this circle builds should guard a **class** of files, not a
list."*

The general form: **an instrument tested against the failures you imagined
certifies your imagination.** Biting each gate at birth proved they detect
tampering *of listed files* — which was never the attack.

## 6. The closure claim failed, and the successor caught it

`the-workshop`'s README: *"It is complete: there is no round 9… **Nothing more
will be added.**"*

Its live HEAD is `e0b09a5`, *"Meta audit + the open-questions ledger,"* dated
after the seal. Circle Two measured this and recorded the correction, dated:

> Its live default branch **has since grown past the seal, additively** —
> measured at Circle Three's round-six adjudication, no deletions and no sealed
> artifact moved. **The seal is a prefix of the live branch, not the whole of
> it.**

This is the single best piece of evidence that the successor-circle design does
real work. A finality claim was falsified, and it was caught **by the next
circle, across a boundary its author had declared final, by measurement rather
than by argument.** No instrument inside Circle One could have caught it,
because Circle One had switched itself off.

## 7. The ratchet, stated as the honest tension

Read forward, the sequence is a system getting steadily better at not
overclaiming and steadily slower at claiming anything.

Circle Four's Q3 returns **"NOT MET, eight for eight,"** enumerates the eight
requirements, plants `fakeCocycle` so a grep cannot mislead, and states two
refusals explicitly rather than leaving them to inference. That is exemplary.
Circle Five then takes **two of three** candidate questions, drops the third,
and re-enters admissibility **from zero**.

Both moves are right. Together they are a ratchet: **the discipline that makes
the record trustworthy is the same discipline that drove the output to zero.**
Nobody chose that trade; it is the sum of four locally-correct decisions.

## 8. What no instance can see — and the cheapest possible fix

Each circle receives a **pinned crate** and its own round. It can verify state
to the byte: hashes match, manifests balance, gates bite. What it cannot see is
**the derivative** — whether ceremony is rising, whether product is falling,
whether the human is fading out of its own record.

Across four repositories, 254 authored documents and roughly 430,000 words, **no
artifact computes a first difference.** Every instrument measures a state
against a claim about that state. Not one measures this circle against the last.

The system built instruments for **state** and none for **rate**, and every
finding in this document lives in the rate.

The fix is embarrassingly small — one carried-forward file, four numbers, one
row per circle:

```
circle | canonical theorems | authored words | ceremony/1k | human-name mentions
One    | 1150               | 143,980        |  7.6        | 62
Two/3  |  269               | 221,893        | 37.6        | 134
Four   |  299               |  44,832        | 47.4        | 5
Five   |    0               |  20,691        | 47.5        | 0
```

Any seat could produce that row for its own circle in one command, and the row
would have told Circle Five at its founding what took an outsider with four
clones to see. **It is the same lesson `COLLABORATION.md` reached from the other
side: put it where a stranger can open it — and note that a stranger who can
open *two* of them sees something neither one contains.**

## Addendum — the fix is built, and it supersedes this document's arithmetic

`rate/rate.py` + `rate/RATE-LEDGER.md`. The four rows now exist and the
instrument prints their first differences.

**Its numbers supersede the tables above**, which were hand-computed with
shell one-liners in the course of noticing. The differences are small and the
direction is identical, but the instrument's are reproducible and defined:

| | words (hand → instrument) | ceremony/1k (hand → instrument) |
|---|---|---|
| One | 143,980 → **148,613** | 7.6 → **7.4** |
| Two+Three | 221,893 → **226,503** | 37.6 → **36.8** |
| Four | 44,832 → **45,755** | 47.4 → **46.4** |
| Five | 20,691 → **21,070** | 47.5 → **46.6** |

(`wc -w` and Python's `.split()` disagree slightly on what a word is. Neither
is wrong; only one is written down. That is the whole argument for the
instrument over the one-liner.)

The differences it prints:

```
One → Two+Three:  theorems 1150 ↓ 269 (-77%)   ceremony/1k 7.4 ↑ 36.8 (+397%)
                  words 148613 ↑ 226503        human mentions 62 ↑ 134
Two+Three → Four: theorems  269 ↑ 299 (+11%)   ceremony/1k 36.8 ↑ 46.4 (+26%)
                  words 226503 ↓ 45755         human mentions 134 ↓ 5 (-96%)
Four → Five:      theorems  299 ↓ 0  (-100%)   ceremony/1k 46.4 ↑ 46.6 (+0%)
                  words  45755 ↓ 21070         human mentions   5 ↓ 0 (-100%)
```

**And the instrument bites itself.** `--selftest` demonstrates that renaming
the governance vocabulary to words outside TERMS v1 makes ceremony density
*fall*, because synonyms still count as words: the metric is not merely
evadable, evading it scores well. So the instrument ships with the rule that
governs it — *if a circle starts steering by these numbers, delete it rather
than refine it* — and with six named things it cannot catch.

**Not installed in any circle.** Copyable, self-contained, adoption is a seat's
decision through its own ceremony. Dropping it into Circle Five's tree from
outside would be the failure this repository documented one turn earlier.
